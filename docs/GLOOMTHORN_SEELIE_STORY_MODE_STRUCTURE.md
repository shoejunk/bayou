# Gloomthorn Seelie Story Mode

Status: implemented route and validation contract

Date: 2026-09-10

## Purpose

The Seelie route teaches all thirteen cards in the Seelie starter while telling one continuous court story from Vesper's bounded Mirror choice in revised Book Two, Chapter 16, through the ordinary-work epilogue in revised Book Three. It does not replay the Book Two investigation already carried by Mirewatch and Blackthorn. The opening explains why Vesper keeps only the Cathedral road; the first tactical entry then gives control as Gloom Fairies attack the Cathedral of Last Lights.

The player should be able to explain four recurring terms without opening a codex:

- A **route** carries movement, information, or authority and can be limited.
- A **bearer** is a person or structure asked to carry part of that authority.
- An **exit** makes protection, service, and alliance revocable.
- A **witness** keeps limits, costs, and corrections public.

The story escalates causally: the Road of Small Lights rescue gives Lash a missing measurement; that measurement helps expose the underroot invasion road; the Empire attacks to force one complete bearer; seven incomplete holders and real vacancies make that system fail.

## Campaign shape

The route has 57 entries: 15 tactical levels and 42 StoryOnly scenes. Story scenes use three to six panels according to the beat instead of forcing unrelated decisions into a synopsis. Chapter 30 remains the largest 8v8 combat capstone; two later canonical objective boards punctuate the climax without turning withdrawal, testimony, surrender, Recall, or medical confirmation into attacks or invented inputs.

The five compact guided rehearsals (`se04`, `se07a`, `se07`, `se10`, and `se13`) are explicitly non-canon optional practice boards. Players read the canon setup first, then may start or skip each without blocking the campaign; skipping grants no gameplay mastery, and the choice warns that later required missions use these rules. `se14` is a required non-canon open bridge: without a prescribed move order, the player must expand an existing 15-square position to 22 controlled squares while preserving Pavo. Exhaustive first-command validation proves that it cannot end before at least one opponent response. It teaches the ordinary territory rule before later canonical battles assume it.

## Difficulty curve

1. Entry 1 is a StoryOnly boundary scene; Entries 2-4 accept only highlighted legal actions and explain immediate cause and effect.
2. Entries 6, 9, 10, 15, and 21 offer compact advanced-rule drills at the point where their characters matter. They are deterministic and skippable.
3. Entry 22 removes highlights on a forgiving three-allies-versus-two-enemies board, asking for the player's first independent target order.
4. Entry 25 raises pressure to a mixed four-versus-four fight and increases the opponent's search depth.
5. Entry 30 adds a diverse five-versus-five combined-arms battle with durable blockers and formation management.
6. Entry 37 changes the win condition from elimination to reaching a marked square through an equal-force position.
7. Entry 44 combines auras, Bodyguard, Control, Disable, ranged attacks, flight, and charges in the largest open formation: an 8v8 defense with Sylvara marked `PROTECT`.
8. Entry 45 changes the task again: Rowan must survive and reach G4 through a 4v4 position; the 311 civilians and gate mechanism remain narrative, never pieces or switches.
9. Entry 54 closes the tactical curve with a 6v7 control objective. Reed and Rowan must survive while the player reaches 28 controlled squares; the HUD reports current progress directly, and no Emperor, captain, archer, clerk, mark, or surrender choice is a kill gate.

The opponent planner scales from search depth 1 in early guided play to depth 2 at `se17`, depth 3 at `se19`/`se23`, and depth 4 at `se27`, `se28a`, and `se29e`.

Open tactical levels use the normal opponent planner, ordinary End Turn flow, and ordinary victory evaluation. They award no deterministic mastery stamp merely for loading a board.

## Narrative boundary

The following are story, never player commands: memory offerings, consent, names, testimony, contracts, votes, custody, harness releases, civilian movement, casualty counts, root cuts, bells, doors, labels, concordance plates, keys, receiver operations, reflected attacks, the Emperor's Recall, and Lash's death.

The Cathedral's Dusk Harvester escapes in panels. Eyeblight escapes the crypt in panels. The Chapter 30 board defends one local sector and contains no Emperor or Lash target. Chapter 35 includes one ordinary square-control board at the witness rail, but the captain's shot, each archer's surrender, Recall, Reed's limited renunciation, the Emperor's confirmed death, and Lash's later medical confirmation remain StoryOnly consequences. No kill objective causes any of those acts.

The Seelie route introduces the consequences of Unseelie decisions but withholds their private point of view. Nyxara, Vespara, Tockman, the Wardens, Gloom Fairies, and the Choir receive their interior story and full mechanical grounding in the later Unseelie route.

## Implementation safeguards

- `StoryCampaign::Seelie` has independent ID-based progress under the `_seelie` save suffix.
- Story role identity follows a legitimate Rebirth replacement on the same square, preventing false death or stale actor IDs.
- Guided scripts are replayed action by action in `storymodetest`.
- Open objectives must have a two-sided authored setup, an empty script, and no unverified mastery award.
- Every panel and card asset is checked with exact-case paths.
- A controlled enemy retains a visible Control badge through its final borrowed turn and never satisfies `DefeatAllEnemies` until its original piece identity leaves the board.
- Story-wrong actions, engine-illegal drags, off-board drops, enemy drags, unready or already-acted units, invalid discards, and blocked Relentless inputs all explain the applicable real rule instead of failing silently.
- Every authored survivor failure condition is generated from role identity, repeated as `MUST SURVIVE: <names>`, and marked directly on each required unit with a gold ring and `PROTECT` label. Failure names the character who fell.
- A single card in hand sits beside a full-width readable action plaque. Multi-card guided copy is measured against the remaining plaque height and shrinks only as needed; survivor names are not redundantly appended inside that narrow copy. The Control rehearsal is placed clear of the top and bottom HUD.
- Narrow 4:3 and 16:10 windows now retain the full 600-unit authored height instead of shrinking the interface into a letterboxed strip. Compact player banners keep Exit/Restart on screen, the duplicate side goal is removed, and primary tutorial/correction copy renders at 16 physical pixels in an 800x600 window.
- The accepted UI-capture baseline includes the selector, first and final mission pages, action/Control briefings, the recommended-drill skip decision, both Act VI recap panels, separate Silkmaw/Pallid scenes, all fifteen tactical boards including the late reach/control objectives, visible Draw/Discard piles, guided/engine correction states, unready input, named survivor failures, and the final Court Behind the Curtain, city-at-night, and Hunger Has No Face art at 1920x1080 and 800x600.
- Every one of the 57 active Seelie scenarios has dedicated 1024x1536 custom art. The final pass adds `se05_court_behind_curtain`, `se22b_city_spends_at_night`, and `se26a_hunger_has_no_face`; their runtime mappings, dimensions, content hashes, and both supported panel layouts are included in the release audit.
